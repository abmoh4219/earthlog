# Responsible use

Earthlog replays real events in which real people were hurt, and every night it replays the world's news. These rules shape what we build and what we accept in contributions.

1. **Truth over drama.**
   - Every narrated sentence cites recorded data or a linked report, and the validator enforces it.
   - No invented facts, no speculation presented as fact, no "bombshell" framing.
   - Estimates and interpolations are labelled.
2. **Measure what the data measures.**
   - Internet signals are connectivity, not people.
   - Fire detections are thermal anomalies, not confirmed damage.
   - "How much the world covered a story" is media attention, not importance or truth.
3. **No tracking of individuals.** No private persons, private aircraft (honour ADS-B privacy programmes when flights arrive), homes or personal devices. Infrastructure, natural phenomena, public events and aggregate signals only. Public figures appear only as the news reports them.
4. **No impersonation.** Earthlog never mimics a news outlet's branding or voice. AI-written narration is labelled as such. Headlines are attributed to their source, with a link.
5. **Respect the sources.** Every dataset and report is credited and linked, its licence is honoured, and its limits are stated.
6. **News of every kind: in scope, cited and neutral** (ADR-0011, changed from "sensors only" by Abdelah on 2026-11-04).
   - Politics, elections, conflict, sports, science, business, culture, health and disasters are all covered.
   - The narrator may state news in its own words, **but every sentence cites a report** that says it.
   - **Contested claims, casualty figures and blame are attributed:** "according to the Associated Press…", "Iran's health ministry said…". Where sources disagree, say so.
   - Neutral language: no "enemy", no loaded terms, the same rules for every side and every party.
   - **No tactical detail:** no live or recent positions of forces, nothing that could help target anyone.
7. **Elections.**
   - Results are shown as **called or projected** races, as recorded by Wikipedia (citing its outlets) at the time of baking, with that time on screen. Never "certified" unless a source says so.
   - Uncalled races are shown as uncalled, not guessed. Both parties' colours are equally strong; flips are highlighted the same way whichever side gains.
8. **People in the news.**
   - Public figures appear as the news and Wikidata record them.
   - **Private individuals** (victims, bystanders, suspects who aren't public figures) are **not named**, even if a source names them.
   - Deaths are told with respect: no mockery, no sensational framing, no images of the dead.
9. **Attention is not importance.** "Most read" means curiosity, not merit or truth. Panels say "readers", never "support" or "popularity", and a language is never presented as a country.
10. **Sports and culture are joyful, but still true.** "Argentina's internet traffic fell 28% during the final" needs a baseline comparison before we suggest the match caused it.
11. **Contributions follow the same rules.** Event pull requests that break these rules are declined, however good they look.

These rules also follow Anthropic's Usage Policy, which applies wherever Claude writes narration.
